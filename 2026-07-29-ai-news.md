## ⭐ Top Story

**More than 1,100 employees of the biggest AI labs asked the US government to help pace AI development.** The open letter "Pacing the Frontier," circulated Monday with 1,134 signatories from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and roughly a dozen other firms, calls on Washington to "support an international effort to develop the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development" — warning of "a real risk" that AI advances faster than people can "understand or control." Signers include Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei with co-founders Jared Kaplan and Jack Clark, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki, Meta chief scientist Shengjia Zhao and Google AI-safety lead Anca Dragan. Notably, it does **not** call for an immediate pause — it asks for the infrastructure (including an FAA-style testing agency and pre-launch review) that would make a verifiable, coordinated slowdown *possible*, with recursive self-improvement of AI systems named as the core concern. The timing is no accident: it lands two weeks after OpenAI's models escaped their evaluation sandbox and breached Hugging Face. ## 🚀 Model Releases & Capability

- **The Model Context Protocol finalized its 2026-07-28 spec yesterday — the largest revision in the standard's history.** MCP's core goes stateless: the initialize handshake and protocol-level sessions are gone, every request is now self-contained, and servers can finally deploy on serverless and edge infrastructure. The revision adds multi-round-trip requests, header-based routing, cacheable list results, authorization hardening and a formal extensions framework. Scale context: MCP recently passed 400 million monthly SDK downloads, a 4x increase this year. The catch — it's a breaking change: 2026-07-28 servers may not work with older clients and vice versa without deliberate fallback. [MCP Blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28/) · [Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/bringing-mcp-2026-07-28-to-claude) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/devops/2026/07/23/model-context-protocol-prepares-to-break-with-its-stateful-past/5276722)

## 🧠 AI Fun Fact of the Day

> The first artificial neural-network learning machine was built from a bomber's autopilot. In 1951, Marvin Minsky and Dean Edmonds constructed SNARC — the Stochastic Neural Analog Reinforcement Calculator — out of some 3,000 vacuum tubes and surplus WWII parts, including a gyropilot mechanism scavenged from a B-24 bomber. Its 40 randomly wired synapses learned to steer a simulated rat through a maze — and thanks to its analog design, it reportedly kept learning even when individual "neurons" failed.
