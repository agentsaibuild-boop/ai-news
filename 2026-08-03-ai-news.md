## ⭐ Top Story

**OpenAI announced its next major model family, Astra — by dropping solutions to ten previously open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science.** Published Friday alongside a 249-page technical manuscript, each result comes with a machine-checkable Lean 4 certificate (public on GitHub under Apache 2.0), so anyone can verify the proofs without waiting for peer review. The headline result is an explicit construction of a non-sofic group — open since Gromov introduced soficity in 1999 — with others spanning von Neumann algebras, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography and sphere packing. OpenAI says the tokens behind all ten solutions would have cost about **$2,000** at Sol's API rates. There is no release date, no decision on whether it ships as GPT-6 — and Astra is set to be the first model to go through Washington's planned pre-release review framework (see Policy below). ## 🏛️ Policy, Regulation & Governance

- **The EU AI Act's biggest deadline arrived yesterday — heavily defanged, but not empty.** August 2 was originally the general application date for high-risk AI obligations, but the AI Omnibus (final Council sign-off June 29, in force since July) pushed Annex III high-risk systems (employment, credit, biometrics, law enforcement) to **December 2, 2027** and product-embedded AI to **August 2, 2028**. What *did* kick in yesterday: Article 50 transparency duties (disclose AI interaction, label AI-generated content — with a grace period to December 2, 2026 for the machine-readable watermarking layer), member-state enforcement machinery and penalties, and — crucially — the Commission's power to actually **fine** general-purpose AI model providers, whose documentation and copyright obligations have applied since 2025 without teeth. Penalties reach €35M or 7% of worldwide turnover. [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/eu-digital-omnibus-ai-enters-force) · [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- **California's AI Transparency Act (SB 942) became operative yesterday too — deliberately aligned with the EU date.** Generative-AI providers with 1M+ monthly California users must now embed provenance disclosures in AI-generated images, video and audio, offer a free public AI-detection tool, and are prohibited from stripping provenance data — with the AB 853 amendments having moved the original January date to August 2. [Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/new-california-ai-laws.html) · [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/california-ai-transparency-law)
- **Washington missed its own AI deadline.** Executive Order 14409's August 1 due date for the voluntary frontier-model review framework passed with no Federal Register notices and no NIST or CISA publications — leaving labs (including OpenAI, whose Astra is meant to be the framework's first customer) without a definition of "covered frontier model." [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/white-house-ai-framework-deadline-002011007.html)

## 🔬 Research & Science

- **Unit 42 documented a real-world autonomous AI cyberattack campaign.** A Chinese-speaking actor ("knaithe") wired DeepSeek into the open-source Hermes Agent framework and directed it via Telegram: in one fully recovered May session, a single instruction was followed by autonomous target enumeration, exploit sourcing from GitHub and attack execution across **460+ internet-facing systems** — with no further operator input. Only three compromises were confirmed (Citrix NetScaler memory exfiltration), and the operation was exposed when the agent carelessly launched a public HTTP server leaking its own loot. Researchers noted Claude and OpenAI safety controls blocked equivalent tasking. ## 🧠 AI Fun Fact of the Day

> Alan Turing wrote a chess program before any computer existed that could run it. Turochamp, written with David Champernowne in 1948, was executed entirely by hand — Turing acted as the CPU, flipping through pages of calculations for about half an hour per move. In its famous 1952 "paper machine" game, it lost to his colleague Alick Glennie in 29 moves.
