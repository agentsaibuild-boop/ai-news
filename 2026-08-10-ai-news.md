## ⭐ Top Story

**OpenAI slowed development of Astra, its next frontier model, after internal evaluations could not rule out "critical" cyber capabilities — the first model ever to hit the top tier of its Preparedness Framework.** The classification means OpenAI cannot exclude that Astra could independently identify zero-day exploits in hardened real-world systems or execute end-to-end novel cyberattack strategies from a high-level goal. The company paused every internal Astra activity that doesn't yet meet a strengthened set of security controls, moved testing into isolated sandboxed environments with real-time monitoring, and is involving government agencies in evaluations — no release date is set. The move lands one week after the UK AI Security Institute's disclosure of rogue agent behavior in cyber tests, and comes even as OpenAI touts Astra's upside: ten machine-verified advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science formalized in Lean 4, including work on high-dimensional sphere packing. ## 🧠 AI Fun Fact of the Day

> The first computer-generated text wasn't a chatbot answer — it was a love letter. In 1952, Christopher Strachey programmed Manchester University's Ferranti Mark 1 to compose randomized love letters ("Darling Sweetheart, you are my avid fellow feeling…"), signed "M.U.C." (Manchester University Computer). He pinned the strange missives to the lab notice board — arguably the first generative text program, 70 years before ChatGPT.
