## 🚀 Model Releases & Capability

- **Alibaba released Qwen3.8-Max — a 2.4-trillion-parameter multimodal MoE — with open weights promised within days.** The model went broadly available yesterday via an OpenAI-compatible API: up to **1M tokens of context** (~991K usable input), text + image + video input, and Qwen's claim that it trails only the strongest frontier models. Open weights for both Max and a companion **Qwen3.8-27B** (the realistic on-premise option) are slated to hit Hugging Face and ModelScope this week — which would make it the largest open-weight model ever shipped. Activated-parameter count, license and full benchmarks are still undisclosed. [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/03/alibaba-qwen-releases-qwen3-8-max/)
- **DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 is official — and it beats DeepSeek's own flagship on agent benchmarks at 1/10th the size.** Released Friday, the re-post-trained 284B/13B-active model scores **82.7 on Terminal-Bench** — about 10 points above the much larger V4-Pro-Preview — at an unchanged **$0.14/$0.28 per million tokens**, with new Responses-API and Codex compatibility. Artificial Analysis now rates it the cheapest well-known model in the world to run: ~$113 to complete its full benchmark suite versus $1,071 for V4 Pro. [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/31/deepseek-upgrades-deepseek-v4-flash-0731-with-major-agentic-and-coding-gains/) · [Quartz](https://qz.com/deepseek-v4-flash-cheapest-ai-model-benchmark-080326)
- **Microsoft's unannounced "MAI-Realtime" voice model surfaced in a hidden preview.** Spotted in the MAI Playground on Saturday, it's a **full-duplex** bidirectional model — it listens, reasons and speaks simultaneously rather than taking turns — can run web search and other tools mid-conversation, and auto-switches between **17 languages**. No model card, pricing or launch date yet. [TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/exclusive-microsoft-tests-new-mai-realtime-voice-model/) · [Windows Forum](https://windowsforum.com/windows-news.4/microsoft-mai-realtime-appears-in-hidden-preview-no-launch-confirmed.441386/)

## 🧠 AI Fun Fact of the Day

> In 1979, the Stanford Cart — Hans Moravec's camera-guided robot — successfully crossed a chair-filled room entirely on its own... in about **five hours**. It inched forward one meter at a time, stopping for 10–15 minutes after each move to process its camera images and re-plan. At the time, this was a celebrated landmark in autonomous navigation; today's robots make the same decisions hundreds of times per second.
